Executive summary

NerdWallet: priced for disruption, but still growing and buying back stock

At ~3x EBITDA the market assumes the auto-insurance gap and AI search break the model. Our bear case is still above the share price

1. Narrative

  • NerdWallet runs comparison and guidance content for credit cards, loans, insurance, banking and small-business finance. It earns referral fees when users take out products.
  • Q3 2026 revenue grew 11% to $239.6m, with strong lending and deposits. But auto-insurance revenue fell $17.4m on lower lead volumes, and full-year profit guidance was cut for the second quarter running.
  • The shares have fallen to about half their 52-week high. At $7.46 the company trades at about 3x EBITDA and under 9x earnings, with net cash.
  • Management bought back $99m of stock in the first nine months of 2026, roughly a fifth of today's market value.
  • We weight a recovery case and an AI-disruption case 50/50, which gives a $13 target. The rating is Buy, but with high uncertainty.

Industry overview

Online financial-product marketing is cyclical and changing with AI search

Volumes follow credit conditions and insurer ad budgets; traffic sources are shifting

1. Market

  • Comparison sites earn more when lenders want growth and insurers want new policies. Both swing with credit and claims cycles.
  • Auto insurers raised marketing spend in 2025–26, but NerdWallet cannot yet monetise several top-10 carriers.
  • Search is changing: AI answers reduce clicks through to publishers, which threatens organic traffic.

2. Tailwinds

▲ Lending recovery

Personal loans, student loans and deposits are all growing strongly in 2026.

▲ Direct relationships

Investment in app users and logged-in members reduces reliance on search.

3. Headwinds

▼ Auto-insurance gap

Carrier and agent partnerships are taking longer than expected. Management said its timelines were 'overly optimistic'.

▼ Paid-marketing cost

Q3 sales & marketing rose 28% to $173m, which halved the operating margin to 8%.

Competitive analysis

A scaled brand in a field of performance marketers

Trades below lead-gen peers on every multiple

1. Positioning: ROE vs revenue

  • Peers such as LendingTree, MediaAlpha, EverQuote and QuinStreet monetise similar traffic. EverQuote and MediaAlpha are more exposed to auto insurance.
  • NerdWallet's brand and editorial reach are its edge, but they depend on search and AI distribution.

2. Porter’s five forces

Rivalry

Intense: comparison sites, bank and insurer direct channels, Credit Karma.

Substitutes

AI assistants answering 'best credit card' questions directly.

New entrants

Low technical barriers; the barrier is brand trust and partner integrations.

Suppliers

Traffic sources (Google, app stores) hold much of the power.

Buyers

Financial providers move budgets quickly between channels based on cost per acquisition.

2. Buybacks are shrinking the share count fast

3. The growth engines outside auto insurance are working
