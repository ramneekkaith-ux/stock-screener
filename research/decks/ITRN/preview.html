Executive summary

Ituran: a subscription compounder priced like a hardware seller

Recurring telematics revenue is growing faster than the market gives it credit for, and it is backed by net cash and a ~4% dividend

1. Narrative

  • Ituran sells stolen-vehicle recovery and connected-car services on monthly subscriptions. Subscriptions were 76% of revenue in Q2 2026 and grew 25% year on year.
  • Its 2.71 million subscribers are spread across Israel, where management claims an 85–90% telematics share, Brazil, other Latin American markets, India and the US.
  • Car-maker (OEM) deals such as Stellantis, BMW Motorrad and Yamaha bring in subscribers without retail marketing spend, and Ituran has added roughly 40,000 a quarter.
  • The business is highly cash-generative: record operating cash flow of $32.2m in Q2 2026, a $10m quarterly dividend, and net cash on the balance sheet.
  • At about 8x EV/EBITDA and 10x EV/EBIT, the stock trades well below telematics and connected-fleet peers (medians ~13x and ~21x) despite strong margins. Our target implies ~20% upside.

Stolen-vehicle recovery, fleet management and connected-car services, sold through insurers, car makers and directly

Industry overview

Vehicle telematics is moving from aftermarket boxes to factory-fitted services

Insurers, car makers and fleets all want connected-vehicle data

1. Market

  • Stolen-vehicle recovery is close to essential in Brazil and Israel, where insurers often require or reward a tracking device.
  • Car makers increasingly fit telematics at the factory and look for a service partner to run it, which is Ituran's OEM channel.
  • Fleet management and usage-based insurance add more data services on top of the same device.

2. Tailwinds

▲ OEM partnerships

Stellantis, BMW Motorrad (Brazil) and Yamaha deals bring subscribers in at the factory, with no retail acquisition cost.

▲ New services on the base

IturanMob for rental fleets launched in Brazil and is piloting in the US (Orlando, New York). Ituran also won its first multi-year big-data traffic-monitoring contract (Sep 2026).

3. Headwinds

▼ Country and FX risk

Most revenue is earned in shekels and reais while Ituran reports in dollars. The swings move both earnings and the share price.

▼ Connected cars

Car makers' own built-in connectivity could, over time, reduce demand for aftermarket recovery devices.

Competitive analysis

A dominant home market and a scale position in Brazil

High returns on equity relative to larger connected-fleet peers

1. Positioning: ROE vs revenue

  • In Israel, management claims an 85–90% telematics share, which makes it the default provider for insurers.
  • Profitability is high for its size: EBIT margins of about 21% and ROE of 27% in 2025.

2. Porter’s five forces

Rivalry

Fragmented in Latin America (local trackers, insurer-owned services); near-monopoly in Israel.

Substitutes

Car makers' built-in connectivity and phone-based tracking are long-term substitutes for aftermarket devices.

New entrants

Low barriers for devices; high barriers for a recovery network (control centres, field teams, police links).

Suppliers

Hardware components are commoditised; cellular connectivity is bought from several carriers.

Buyers

Insurers and OEMs negotiate hard on price, but switching a large installed base is costly.

Investment thesis 1

1. Subscriptions are compounding faster than the multiple implies

The fastest-growing part of revenue is also the highest-margin part

The argument

  • Subscription revenue grew 25% year on year in Q2 2026 and is now 76% of revenue. This is recurring income with high visibility.
  • Net adds have held at about 40,000 a quarter for over a year, with OEM channels adding to the base.
  • Profit is growing faster than revenue: in Q2, EBITDA rose 24% and net income 29% on revenue up 21%.

What would prove us wrong

Net adds slow well below 30,000 a quarter, or subscription growth falls back to single digits.

Investment thesis 3

3. New growth levers are optional upside

India, IturanMob in the US and data contracts are not in our numbers

The argument

  • IturanMob, a platform for small and mid-sized rental companies, is piloting in Orlando and New York after launching in Brazil.
  • The Mercedes-Benz commercial-vehicle joint venture in India is low-margin today but long-dated.
  • The September 2026 big-data traffic-monitoring contract shows that the subscriber data has value beyond recovery.

What would prove us wrong

These projects eat margin without reaching scale. Management has called India a long-term, low-margin bet.

What our base case leaves out

  • Our DCF uses consensus growth of ~16% in 2026 and ~5% in 2027, fading to 2.5%. It does not assume any US or India success.
  • Any sign that IturanMob or the data business is gaining scale would be upside to the target.
