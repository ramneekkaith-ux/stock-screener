2. Cash returns put a floor under the stock

A ~4% dividend yield and net cash protect the downside

The argument

  • Ituran pays a $10m quarterly dividend ($0.50 per share), about a 4% yield at $48, and buys back shares as well.
  • Operating cash flow reached a record $32.2m in Q2 2026, comfortably covering the dividend.
  • Ituran holds net cash, so the dividend does not depend on borrowing, and there is room for acquisitions, which management prefers for expansion.

What would prove us wrong

A large debt-funded acquisition, or a dividend cut to fund growth outside the core.
